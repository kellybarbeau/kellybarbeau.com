Room Refresh: interior design without the designer

Process designConnected toolsReusable toolkit

Redesigning five rooms usually means hiring an interior designer: thousands of dollars and months of revisions before anything gets bought. I built a process that let me do it myself. It took days to mock up every room and a few weeks to buy, paint and install.

  1. 1

    Gather taste

    Pulls a saved inspiration board straight from the source and turns hundreds of images into a shared style brief.

  2. 2

    Shoot and measure

    Phone photos go in as-is and convert automatically. A short shoot guide gets the AI the angles it needs.

  3. 3

    Mock up one change at a time

    A script sends each photo and a plain-English change to an image model's API, and saves every version next to the instructions that made it.

  4. 4

    Turn decisions into a plan

    Approved looks become a shopping list with real products, vendors, budget ranges and the trades to book.

  5. 5

    Hand it off

    Packaged as Room Refresh Starter, a playbook and prompt library anyone can run on their own home.

What I learned: most of the value isn't in the model. It's in connecting the pieces (inspiration, photos, AI, vendors, budget) into one process a non-expert can follow.

A picture book, written and illustrated with AI

Creative productionCharacter consistencyPrint-ready output

A one-of-a-kind, 36-page rhyming picture book made as a family Father's Day gift: the story of a tiny barnacle looking for her rock, with characters based on our daughter's real stuffed animals. It went from idea to a hardcover printed through Blurb.

  • A written character bible, style guide and page-by-page storyboard keep 18 illustrations consistent.
  • A script connects reference photos of the stuffed animals to an image model's API, then routes approved pages through upscaling and into the print vendor's exact layout specs.

What I learned: consistency comes from writing the rules down first, not from a clever prompt.
